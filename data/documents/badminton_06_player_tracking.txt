Badminton Player Tracking

Player tracking connects player detections across consecutive video frames. Instead of treating every detection as a new person, a tracking algorithm assigns a consistent identity to each player while they move around the court.

Multi-object tracking methods such as ByteTrack can be combined with object detectors to maintain player trajectories. In badminton analysis, tracking is commonly used to follow the two players during a rally.

The resulting trajectory can contain the player's position over time. This information can be transformed into court coordinates when the camera view and court geometry are known.

Player tracking enables higher-level measurements such as distance covered, movement speed, court coverage, and spatial positioning. It can also support tactical analysis by showing how players move between offensive and defensive positions.

Player tracking is different from shuttlecock tracking. Player tracking follows relatively large human objects, while shuttlecock tracking follows a very small and fast-moving object. A complete badminton analysis system may use separate models for both tasks.
