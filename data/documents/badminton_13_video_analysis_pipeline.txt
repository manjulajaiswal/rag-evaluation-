Badminton Video Analysis Pipeline

A badminton video-analysis pipeline combines several computer-vision stages to convert raw match footage into useful sports information.

A typical pipeline begins with video input and may first detect the badminton court and players. Player detection can then be combined with a tracking algorithm to maintain player identities across frames.

In parallel, a shuttlecock detector or TrackNet-based model can identify and track the shuttlecock. Pose estimation can provide body keypoints for the players.

The extracted information can then be transformed into court coordinates using court calibration or homography. Temporal processing can identify hits, rallies, or other events.

The final stage can generate statistics and visualizations. Examples include player movement, shuttle trajectories, rally duration, shot information, and court coverage.

The pipeline is modular because each stage solves a different problem. A failure in shuttle tracking does not necessarily mean that player detection has failed. Similarly, accurate player tracking does not guarantee accurate hit detection.

Separating these stages makes the system easier to evaluate and improve.
