Badminton Hit Detection

Hit detection attempts to identify the moment when a player strikes the shuttlecock with the racket. Detecting hits is useful because hits divide a badminton rally into individual shot events.

A hit-detection system can use information from the shuttlecock trajectory, player position, body pose, and changes in shuttle movement. The exact frame in which a hit occurs can be recorded as a temporal event.

Open-source badminton research projects have created annotations containing hit frames, player information, shuttlecock coordinates, and related video features. These annotations can be used to train or evaluate models for automatic hit detection.

Hit detection is closely related to shuttlecock tracking but represents a different task. Tracking attempts to estimate the shuttlecock position across frames, while hit detection identifies an important event involving the player and shuttlecock.

Reliable hit detection can support rally segmentation, shot classification, trajectory analysis, and automated match statistics.
