Badminton Video Analysis Using Computer Vision

Computer vision can be used to analyze badminton match videos and automatically extract information about players, the shuttlecock, court movement, and events during a rally. A typical badminton video-analysis pipeline combines several computer-vision components rather than relying on a single model.

One part of the system detects and tracks the shuttlecock across video frames. Because the shuttlecock is small and moves quickly, tracking it requires models designed for small and fast-moving objects. TrackNet-based approaches are commonly used for this task.

Another part of the system detects and tracks players. Object detection models such as YOLO can identify players in individual frames, while tracking algorithms can maintain player identities across consecutive frames. Pose estimation can additionally provide body-joint information for analyzing player movement.

The extracted information can then be used for higher-level analysis. Examples include detecting when a player hits the shuttlecock, identifying rally events, estimating shuttle trajectories, measuring player movement, and analyzing court positioning.

A complete badminton analysis pipeline may therefore contain shuttle detection, player detection, tracking, pose estimation, event detection, and court analysis as separate stages.
